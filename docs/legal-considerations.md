# Legal considerations for the shared ledger

Udhaar records arrangements and user-reported actions. The agreed paid-lending
feature will record externally made payments without receiving, transferring,
or holding funds. It takes no commission and does not offer public item listings
or matching. These product choices narrow the review; they do not create a
nationwide exemption from legal obligations.

This is preliminary research as of November 11, 2026, not a legal opinion.
The live [Terms](../TERMS.md) and [Privacy notice](../PRIVACY.md) describe the
current item-tracking beta. Planned financial metadata requires a policy review
before release.

## Records and real-world events

A user entering a payment reports an event. It does not establish bank verification
or the other person's agreement. Likewise, possession of a guest link does not
independently establish the actor's identity.

Preserve the distinction between reported payment, acknowledged receipt,
calculated balance, and corrections. A settled balance should remain explainable
from its underlying history. Deletion and retention require separate handling.

Describe records accurately. Avoid promising that an app acknowledgment creates
a universally enforceable agreement, or that an app entry extinguishes an
underlying obligation. Such effects depend on the facts and applicable law.

## Splitwise comparison

Splitwise's [terms](https://www.splitwise.com/terms) describe a collaborative
informal ledger and disclaim the accuracy of user entries. Its current help
explains that [manual settlement creates a payment record that can be deleted to
restore the balance](https://kb.splitwise.com/balances-and-expenses/i-accidentally-settled-up-how-can-i-undo-this).
[Activity](https://kb.splitwise.com/account-issues/where-can-i-view-a-history-of-recent-changes-to-my-account)
provides a history of changes, with only the most recent 100 actions shown in the
mobile activity list.

A [2015 support response](https://feedback.splitwise.com/forums/162446-general/suggestions/10672368-need-approval-of-spender-during-settlement)
explained its decision against mandatory payment approval. That is historical
product context, not a rule requiring Udhaar to behave the same way.
Splitwise's payment services have separate terms and are outside this comparison.

Udhaar currently requires the lender to confirm shared returns. For future payment
records, confirmation, dispute, correction, and history rules remain design work.
A competitor's terms do not establish Udhaar's legal position.

## Rental income and taxes

The [IRS guidance on rental income](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc414) distinguishes
personal-property rentals conducted as a business from other rentals.
A receipt summary can help users keep records; it should not claim to calculate
profit or taxable income.

States treat rentals under their own rules. Examples include
[Texas sales and use tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/),
[California rental guidance](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/rental-companies/leases-in-general.htm),
and [New Jersey leases and rentals](https://nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/pubs/sales/su12.pdf).
Those sources establish issues for rental transactions, not a determination that
Udhaar itself must collect tax.

The [IRS Form 1099-K overview](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-k)
addresses payment settlement entities. A manual ledger entry differs from
settling a payment. Review the actual role before introducing integrations;
do not infer reporting duties or exemptions merely from transaction volume.

## Privacy and communications

Names, contact details, private notes, and planned payment metadata require
appropriate access controls, security, retention, and a private request channel.
The [FTC privacy and security guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security)
is a starting point for accurate notices and reasonable practices.

Assess invitation content separately from operational messages under
[FTC CAN-SPAM guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).
A free service can still send promotional messages.

Age eligibility and handling known children require review under
[FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).
Do not claim a blanket age policy resolves all requirements.

## Before releasing paid records

Identify where the operator actually operates and assess the intended use and
relevant jurisdictions. General US availability is a product audience, not an
operator domicile.

Review public terms and privacy for actual data collected, payment attribution,
corrections/disputes, contact channels, retention, and verified deletion/export.
Keep evidence sufficient to explain records without promising permanent retention.
Revisit the analysis if payments, money lending, public listings, automated fees,
or generated agreements enter scope.
